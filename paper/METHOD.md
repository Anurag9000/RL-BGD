# Method

This document describes the method actually implemented in RL-BGD. It is a
method specification, not an empirical claim. Hyperparameter choices and
benchmark outcomes are reported only from canonical run artifacts.

## 1. Continual reinforcement-learning setting

The primary setting is a continuing agent interacting with a non-stationary
stream of continuous-control environments. In strict task-agnostic experiments
the learner may use only ordinary interaction data:

- observation;
- chosen action;
- reward;
- terminated/truncated signal;
- its own recurrent state where recurrence is enabled;
- replay metadata generated by the learner itself.

It does not receive ground-truth task ID, task name, switch time, environment
context, or evaluator-owned change labels. Canonical Continual World and
oracle-boundary baselines are retained as separate, explicitly privileged
protocols rather than being called task-agnostic.

## 2. Diagonal Gaussian posterior

For every Bayesianized module, RL-BGD maintains a fully factorized Gaussian

    q(theta) = product_i Normal(theta_i | mu_i, sigma_i^2).

Posterior means and standard deviations are stored in FP32 regardless of the
surrounding model precision. Each posterior also stores its initialization
prior `(mu_0, sigma_0)` for exact controlled tempering. Standard deviations
are checked for finiteness and clamped to configured positive bounds.

A parameter sample is reparameterized as

    theta_i^(k) = mu_i + sigma_i epsilon_i^(k),
    epsilon_i^(k) ~ Normal(0, 1).

The implementation supports arbitrary Monte Carlo sample counts; antithetic
sampling is optional and requires an even count.

## 3. BGD update

For sampled gradients `g_i^(k)`, define

    g_bar_i = mean_k g_i^(k)

and the uncertainty/curvature signal

    c_i = mean_k g_evidence,i^(k) epsilon_i^(k).

The update is

    mu_i <- mu_i - eta sigma_i^2 g_bar_i

    sigma_i <- sigma_i sqrt(1 + (0.5 sigma_i c_i)^2)
               - 0.5 sigma_i^2 c_i.

The ordinary mean-learning gradient and the uncertainty/evidence gradient are
explicitly separate channels. In vanilla BGD they are identical. This
separation is what allows replay to keep training the posterior mean while
changing how much repeated data are allowed to consolidate posterior
uncertainty.

On diagonal quadratic objectives, tests verify the expected local relation

    E[g_i epsilon_i] approximately H_ii sigma_i

at sufficiently small uncertainty, including the sign of sigma changes under
positive and negative curvature.

## 4. Generalized-Bayes interpretation and evidence temperature

SAC and PPO surrogate objectives are not literal supervised-data negative log
likelihoods. RL-BGD therefore treats Bayesian RL updates as generalized-Bayes
updates using the RL surrogate as a loss/energy. Conceptually,

    q_u = argmin_q E_q[L_RL,u(theta)]
                    + (1 / beta_u) KL(q || q_prior,u).

The implementation exposes a positive `evidence_temperature = beta`. Before
autodifferentiation, both the mean-learning and uncertainty-evidence objectives
are multiplied by beta. The logged unscaled RL loss remains comparable across
temperature settings.

Temperature and the BGD step scale `eta` are intentionally distinct. Both
affect posterior-mean movement, but beta also directly scales the curvature
signal driving sigma. Controlled tests demonstrate configurations with matched
mean movement but different uncertainty contraction. The paper suite therefore
contains a matched evidence-temperature sweep rather than treating beta as a
renamed learning rate.

## 5. Controlled posterior tempering

Before a Bayesian evidence update, the current posterior may be mixed back
toward its initialization prior through the normalized product

    q_tilde(theta) proportional to
        q_prev(theta)^lambda p_0(theta)^(1-lambda),

with `lambda in [0, 1]`. For each diagonal component, using precisions
`tau = sigma^-2`,

    tau_tilde = lambda tau + (1-lambda) tau_0

    mu_tilde = [lambda tau mu
                + (1-lambda) tau_0 mu_0] / tau_tilde

    sigma_tilde = tau_tilde^(-1/2).

Thus `lambda=1` retains the current posterior and smaller lambda reintroduces
prior uncertainty and prior mean information. Tempering occurs before the
corresponding BGD evidence update.

Fixed-retention experiments isolate the effect of controlled forgetting from
change detection. Adaptive experiments compute lambda online from surprise.

The late-life plasticity stress study isolates posterior-history effects more
strictly. Vanilla and tempered conditions differ only during a long pre-shift
consolidation phase. After the abrupt optimum shift, both conditions force
retention to 1.0 and use the same BGD update rule. The study records inherited
sigma/effective learning rate, first-step parameter movement, normalized
post-shift loss AUC, final target loss, and recovery fraction. This makes a
post-shift mobility difference attributable to the posterior state produced by
the earlier evidence history rather than continued tempering after the shift.

## 6. Surprise-driven replasticization

For a non-negative smoothed surprise signal `S_bar_t`, adaptive retention is

    lambda_t = lambda_min
               + (1-lambda_min) exp(-kappa S_bar_t).

At zero surprise the posterior is fully retained; increasing surprise
monotonically approaches `lambda_min`.

Exactly one adaptive source may control retention in a BGD-SAC run:

1. **TD surprise.** Bellman residual magnitudes are aggregated, normalized by
   exponential running center/variance estimates, and smoothed online.
2. **Twin-critic disagreement.** Variance between critic predictions is used
   as an epistemic-disagreement statistic.
3. **Predictive NLL.** An online Gaussian model predicts next observation and
   reward from the current observation/action. The current batch is scored
   before the dynamics model is trained on that batch, preventing the surprise
   signal from benefiting from same-batch fitting.

Surprise normalizers and the predictive model/optimizer are checkpointed. True
environment change times may be used after the fact for detection metrics but
are never inputs to adaptive training.

## 7. Replay as optimization versus replay as Bayesian evidence

Off-policy replay means the same transition can be sampled repeatedly. RL-BGD
does not assume every reuse is an independent Bayesian observation. The
ordinary replay objective always remains available for posterior-mean
optimization, while the uncertainty loss can use one of four evidence modes:

- **all_replay:** every sampled use has evidence weight 1;
- **fresh_only_uncertainty:** only first sampled use contributes to sigma;
- **inverse_reuse_weight:** uncertainty evidence is weighted by
  `1 / usage_count`;
- **normalized_batch_evidence:** within-batch composition is unchanged but the
  complete uncertainty update is scaled by the batch mean inverse-use weight.

The weighted uncertainty objective is the mean of
`weight_j * loss_j`, deliberately allowing total Bayesian evidence to shrink.
It is not normalized back to a fixed evidence budget. Fresh fraction, usage
counts, evidence weights, and effective sample size are logged.

These modes are experimental evidence-accounting hypotheses, not asserted
Bayesian identities.

## 8. BGD-SAC

RL-BGD supports `critic_only`, `actor_only`, and
`actor_and_critic` Bayesianization.

### Critic update

The SAC Bellman target is formed first from the current policy at mean
parameters and deterministic target critics synchronized from posterior means.
Each sampled Bayesian critic is trained against this fixed target. Target
critics do not maintain independent posterior uncertainty in the default
method; they receive ordinary Polyak updates from live posterior means.

### Actor update

When the actor is Bayesianized, sampled actor parameters are evaluated with
functional calls. For sampled action `a`, the objective is the ordinary SAC
surrogate

    alpha log pi(a | s) - min(Q1(s,a), Q2(s,a)).

Critics are fixed functions for this actor update. The entropy-temperature
parameter alpha remains an ordinary scalar Adam update.

### Replay and adaptive controls

BGD-SAC is the main location where replay-evidence accounting, fixed posterior
tempering, generalized-Bayes temperature, and TD/ensemble/predictive adaptive
retention are combined. Their configurations are orthogonal where scientifically
meaningful; multiple adaptive surprise sources cannot silently control the same
update.

## 9. BGD-PPO

Feed-forward and recurrent PPO share the standard clipped policy surrogate,
GAE, value loss/value clipping, entropy term, minibatch updates, and frozen
behavior-policy log probabilities/values collected before optimization.

Bayesianization may target actor, value network, or both. Parameter samples are
used only inside the update objective; collection does not resample a new
behavior policy at every optimization pass.

Repeated PPO epochs reuse the same rollout and therefore constitute repeated
evidence. BGD-PPO exposes explicit evidence modes, with
`first_epoch_only` as the conservative default and additional all-epoch and
normalized-epoch controls.

## 10. Recurrent hidden-context agents

Recurrent PPO and SAC use matched Adam and BGD architecture families so
recurrence and Bayesian consolidation can be separated experimentally.

The learner receives observation plus its ordinary previous-transition context
where configured: previous action, reward, and done. It never receives the
hidden task/context value.

Recurrent hidden state is reset only on natural episode boundaries
(`terminated` or `truncated`), not on hidden task switches. Rollout/replay
storage records behavior hidden states and episode-start masks. Sequence
training uses explicit burn-in/unroll or truncated-BPTT chunks. For recurrent
PPO, old log probabilities and values remain the frozen behavior quantities.

The 3RL-style Continual World runner preserves the task-agnostic information
contract and key history/replay settings of the source protocol while
explicitly documenting architectural and modern Meta-World deviations.

## 11. Comparison baselines

The repository includes independent implementations of:

- EWC;
- online EWC;
- Synaptic Intelligence;
- MAS;
- oracle-boundary UCL-PPO;
- an exact diagonal FOO-VB/BGD equivalence reference.

Task-agnostic regularization baselines use fixed optimizer-update consolidation
when true task boundaries are unavailable. Boundary-triggered versions are
separately labelled oracle/privileged. This distinction is part of the method,
not merely presentation metadata. The paper registry contains a five-seed
baseline suite for EWC, Online-EWC, SI, MAS, and separately labelled
oracle-boundary UCL-PPO; runnable configurations are not treated as empirical
results until canonical raw artifacts exist.

## 12. Benchmark protocols

### Synthetic controlled streams

Quadratic streams, hidden-context Gaussian bandits, and non-stationary LQR
provide known optima/context schedules for mechanism tests. True context is
evaluator-only under strict runs.

### CARL

CARL Pendulum is used with abrupt, smooth, and recurring hidden-context
schedules. The agent-facing adapter strips context fields while evaluator
metadata remains available separately.

### Continual World

Two protocols are maintained:

- canonical task-aware CW10/CW20 for comparison with the published benchmark;
- strict task-agnostic CW10/CW20 with task identity and boundary callbacks
  removed.

A recurrent 3RL-style task-agnostic path is separate from both. Full benchmark
runs retain the published-scale one-million steps per occurrence unless a
configuration is explicitly labelled smoke/dev.

### ContinualBench and CORA

ContinualBench has a strict hidden-task adapter and isolated pinned live smoke.
CORA compatibility covers canonical protocol metadata and metric definitions.
Its legacy Atari runtime is live-validated in a separate pinned Python 3.10
workflow, keeping obsolete Gym/ALE dependencies isolated rather than forcing
them into the primary environment.

## 13. Evaluation

Implemented continual metrics include final average performance, forgetting,
backward transfer, reference-relative forward transfer, lifetime AUC,
plasticity retention, T80/T90 recovery, post-change AUC, recurrence/reacquisition
metrics, surprise-event detection statistics, and surprise AUROC.

Benchmark-specific definitions remain separate from generic metrics; for
example, generic forward transfer is not substituted for Continual World's
canonical transfer metric.

Mechanistic analyses measure:

- curvature versus BGD uncertainty signal;
- posterior sigma versus future parameter movement;
- posterior precision versus perturbation sensitivity;
- low-/high-sigma freezing counterfactuals;
- uncertainty-quality diagnostics.

## 14. Statistical and artifact protocol

Curated paper suites declare target callable, resolved kwargs, seeds, algorithm,
environment, information protocol, config source, primary/secondary metrics,
and runtime class.

Successful runs are converted automatically to canonical
`manifest.json/config.yaml/metrics.csv/summary.json` directories. Failed or
partial runs remain visible and cause strict paper aggregation to fail rather
than being silently discarded.

Across independent seeds, scalar metrics receive percentile bootstrap
confidence intervals. Paired method differences resample matched-seed
differences. Task-within-seed values use hierarchical seed-then-task bootstrap.
Generated tables preserve contributing run IDs and are exported to CSV,
Markdown, and LaTeX; figures and their source hashes are recorded in
`paper_manifest.json`.

## 15. What this method does not assume

RL-BGD does not assume that:

- SAC/PPO losses are literal likelihoods;
- replay uses are independent Bayesian observations;
- low posterior sigma is automatically a calibrated measure of functional
  importance;
- posterior weight sampling is exactly PSRL;
- a hidden task switch may reset recurrent state;
- a runnable benchmark constitutes an empirical result.

These are either explicit modeling choices or hypotheses tested by the
experiment plan.
